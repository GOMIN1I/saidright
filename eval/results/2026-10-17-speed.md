# Where the wait goes, and what makes it shorter — 2026-10-17

Apple M4 Pro, 24 GB, macOS 27.0. Whisper large-v3-turbo (MLX, fp16) through `Resources/recognizer.py`, RU+EN language set like the owner's modes.

## 1. Breakdown of a real session (owner's log, after the encoder cache)

| Step | Short phrase (2–6 s of speech) | Long dictation (13–27 s) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Whisper after the stop | 0.46–0.56 s | 2.0–3.1 s |
| Text editing (OpenAI `gpt-6-luna`, reasoning off) | 1.0–1.5 s | 1.0–2.5 s |
| Paste (text is visible here) | ~0.02 s | ~0.02 s |
| Verification + clipboard restore (after the text is visible) | 0.2 s | 0.2 s |

So the “two seconds” of a short phrase are mostly the cloud editing step, not Whisper. With a mode whose cleanup is off (e.g. Practice) a short phrase is ready in ≈0.5 s.

Network: a cold HTTPS connection to `api.openai.com` costs ≈0.12–0.18 s of TLS before the request (curl, three runs: tls 0.117–0.184 s).

## 2. Tried and rejected: smaller encoder window

Whisper always encodes a 30 s window, even for a 3 s phrase. Encoding only the audio (plus a margin) was measured on 37 synthetic clips (`eval/fixtures.json`, `eval/routing-fixtures.json`) and the owner's 41 real recordings:

| Variant | WER (synthetic) | median s | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 30 s window (current) | 3.56 % | 0.42 | |
| 8-bit weights | 3.56 % | 0.44 | no gain: not bandwidth-bound |
| 4-bit weights | 4.75 % | 0.44 | worse, no gain |
| window = audio + 1 s, min 10 s, plain transcribe | 12.8 % | 0.16 | repetition loops |
| same, one decode + timestamp cut | 5.34 % | 0.15 | translates mixed phrases |
| min 15 s window | 3.86 % | 0.21 | real recordings: 18/41 differ, incl. **translation** (“My favorite food – pizza” → Russian) and **corrected learner errors** (“My mom are…” → “is”) |
| min 20 s window | 3.56 % | 0.28 | |

Rejected: Whisper was trained on 30 s windows; shorter ones make it translate between the expected languages and “fix” the grammar a learner needs to see. Both are unacceptable for this app.

## 3. Shipped: recognition ahead of the stop (long dictations)

While the person speaks, the app asks the worker every 2 s (after the first 6 s) to recognise phrases that are already finished — followed by at least 1.0 s of pause, in chunks of at least 4 s — from the WAV that is still being written. After the stop only the remainder is recognised.

Simulated on the owner's 16 recordings longer than 6 s (file grown in 2.5 s steps, then the final request):

| Setting | Whisper after the stop, mean | word difference vs. one pass |
| --- | --- | --- |
| one pass (before) | 3.06 s | — |
| cut at 0.6 s pauses, 2 s chunks | 0.40 s | 12.5 % (lost words when a sentence was split) |
| **cut at 1.0 s pauses, 4 s chunks (shipped)** | **0.72 s** | 9.5 %, almost all on two noisy clips (TV audio, mumbling) where the one-pass result itself changes between runs; sentence-level dictations match except punctuation/casing |

In the app (QA run, 27.2 s real recording as test audio): `asr=1.23s` vs 2.75 s before.

## 4. Shipped: warm connection

When a recording starts in a mode that will call a cloud provider, the app sends a HEAD request without credentials to the provider's host, so DNS/TCP/TLS are done while the person speaks (≈0.15 s saved per session after an idle period). Nothing is billed or sent.

## 5. Other recognisers

- **Qwen3-ASR 0.6B** — measured 2026-10-18 (`2026-10-18-qwen3-asr.md`): 0.09–0.12 s per short phrase (4× faster), but it translates whole sentences between Russian and English in mixed speech and is less accurate in Russian. Rejected.
- **Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3** is already built in (≈0.1 s) but drops the other language in mixed speech.
